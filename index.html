Account types and what the letters mean

AccountWhat it is and how the planner taxes it
Traditional IRAIndividual Retirement Account. Money went in before tax, so every dollar withdrawn is ordinary income. Required minimum distributions (RMDs) start at 73.
401(k), 403(b), 457(b)Retirement plans run by an employer: private companies (401k), schools and nonprofits (403b), and government bodies (457b). Taxed like a Traditional IRA, with RMDs from 73.
SEP-IRASimplified Employee Pension, a retirement account for self-employed people. Taxed like a Traditional IRA.
Roth IRAFunded with money already taxed. Qualified withdrawals are tax-free and there are no RMDs for the owner. The planner never taxes it.
HSAHealth Savings Account for medical costs. Tax-free in, tax-free growth, tax-free out for medical spending. The planner never taxes it.
Brokerage (stocks and funds)An ordinary investment account. Dividends are taxed each year at the capital gains rates. A gain is taxed only when shares are sold, and only the part above the cost basis counts.
Savings accountInterest is taxed each year as ordinary income. Selling or withdrawing creates no further tax.
CD (Certificate of Deposit) and bondsA CD is a bank deposit locked for a fixed term. Bonds are loans you make to a company or government. Both pay interest, taxed each year as ordinary income. Interest on municipal bonds can be tax-free, which the planner does not model.
Trust accountInvestments held in a trust. The planner taxes it like a brokerage account. Real trusts can face higher rates.
PropertyTracked in net worth only. It is not drawn on until you add a house sale as a cash event.

Other abbreviations

AGIAdjusted Gross Income, the total taxable income before the standard deduction.
MFJMarried Filing Jointly, the status used while both people are alive.
LTCGLong-term capital gain, profit on an investment held more than a year.
NIITNet Investment Income Tax, an extra 3.8% for higher incomes.
RMDRequired Minimum Distribution, the amount you must take from tax-deferred accounts each year.
COLACost-of-Living Adjustment, the yearly rise in Social Security.
RSURestricted Stock Unit, shares an employer grants that count as wages when they vest.
IRMAAMedicare surcharge for higher incomes. Not modelled.

How interest, dividends and gains are taxed here

Each year. Interest and dividends earned in a taxable account are added to income and taxed, even when they stay invested. The tax is paid from your cash flow, and the money stays in the account.

When you sell. The planner tracks each account's cost basis. If spending forces a sale, the share of the sale above the basis is a gain, taxed at 0%, 15% or 20% depending on total taxable income. It draws a little extra to cover that tax.

Stacking. Gains sit on top of ordinary income. Ordinary income fills the 0% gain band first, so a retiree with little other income can often sell shares at a 0% federal rate.

Social Security. Dividends and gains raise provisional income, so they can make more of the Social Security taxable.

Not modelled. The stepped-up basis at death, losses used to offset gains, state tax on gains different from the flat state rate, the alternative minimum tax, tax-free municipal bond interest, Medicare IRMAA surcharges, and the 3.8% tax on rental income.

Gift Tax Rules (2026)

Annual exclusion: $19,000 per recipient per year. Gifts up to this amount require no reporting.

Lifetime exemption: $15,000,000 per person (2026). Gift tax is rarely owed below this cumulative threshold.

Direct tuition payments to educational institutions are excluded from gift tax entirely (any amount).

Medical payments directly to providers are also excluded.

Required Minimum Distributions

Begin at age 73 (SECURE 2.0 Act, 2023). Calculated from the IRS Uniform Lifetime Table. Formula: account balance ÷ life expectancy factor. Applies to Traditional IRA, 401k, 403b, 457b, SEP-IRA. A 25% penalty applies on any shortfall not withdrawn.

Cash Events — Tax Overview

Event typeTax treatment
Inheritance receivedIncome-tax-free to recipient. Inherited investments get a stepped-up cost basis.
House sale (primary)Up to $500k excluded from capital gains (married, lived there 2 of last 5 years).
Business saleCapital gains if stock sale; ordinary income if asset sale. Get a CPA involved.
RSU / equity vestingTaxed as ordinary income (W-2) in the year received.
Insurance payoutLife insurance death benefits are usually tax-free.
Gift receivedNot taxable to recipient. Giver files a return if over $19k/person/year.
Charitable donationDeductible up to 60% of AGI for cash gifts to qualifying charities when itemising.
Legal settlementPhysical injury awards generally tax-free; punitive/employment awards taxable.
Large purchasesNo deduction. Directly reduces account balances.
Home renovationNo immediate deduction. Increases property cost basis, reducing future capital gains.
Moving costsGenerally not deductible for retirees under current law.
