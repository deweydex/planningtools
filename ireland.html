Auto-enrolment (MyFutureFund) from 1 Jan 2026, employees 23 to 60 earning over €20,000. Employee and employer each pay 1.5% (2026 to 2028), 3% (2029 to 2031), 4.5% (2032 to 2034) and 6% (from 2035), on pay up to €80,000. The State adds €1 for every €3 the employee pays.

Retirement: 25% of the pot is taken as a lump sum (first €200,000 free, next €300,000 at 20%, above €500,000 at the marginal rate). The rest becomes an ARF with a minimum yearly withdrawal of 4% to age 70, 5% from 71 and 6% above €2 million.

State Pension starts at the age you set (66 by default) at the weekly rate in Settings. €10 a week more from age 80. A living alone increase of €25 a week applies once one partner has passed.

The first €3,000 of gifts from any one person each year is exempt and does not use the threshold. Gifts between spouses are free of tax. The thresholds are not indexed by law. The planner assumes they rise with the yearly rise in bands and credits (Settings) so that gifts entered in future euro are treated fairly. Set that rise to 0 for fixed thresholds.

Figures to check ⚠

These come from news and budget summaries, not an official page, or are assumptions. Check them before relying on a result.

  • Budget 2027 figures are as announced and need the Finance Bill to pass.
  • The age credit for 2027 (€245 single, €490 married). Budget 2027 summaries do not mention a change, so the planner keeps it.
  • The Investment Account design. It is announced for 1 July 2027 and is not yet law. The planner does not enforce the €12,000 yearly contribution limit.
  • First Home Scheme: no extension past June 2027 has been announced, and ceilings change every six months.
  • An Employee PRSI rise of 0.2% reported from October 2028 is not modelled. The planner holds the rate at 4.5% from October 2027.
  • The 4% / 5% / 6% minimum ARF rule and the 4-year Help to Buy look-back are modelled in simplified form.

Not modelled

Local Property Tax, rental income, capital gains tax on shares and property, Fair Deal nursing home costs, AMRF rules for smaller pots, mortgage interest rate changes, and tax on a survivor's inheritance.
